Hijri Calendar

Each month begins the day after the crescent is reported in Morocco on the evening of the 29th. If it isn't reported, the month runs to 30 days. Past dates are the official announcements. Upcoming dates, through 1460 AH (2039), are forecasts from a model that learned from 137 past sighting evenings (October 2015 to October 2026) and the crescent maps in your moon folder.

Learning from past results

How the forecast is made and how good it is

Latest data added All of 1437 AH (Oct 2015 to Sep 2016) plus 11 months that were missing from 1438, 1439 and 1445, for 23 new evenings. The record is now complete from Muharram 1437 to Jumada al-Ula 1448. The 1445 tab now shows that year's calendar and its predicted-vs-actual cards: the walk-forward model called all 12 of its month starts. On the same 94 test evenings as before, accuracy rose from 93.6% to 94.7% and the Brier score fell from 0.052 to 0.044. On the borderline ones, accuracy rose from 77% to 81%.

What the model weighs

It reads each 29th-evening map over Moroccan land, from Tangier to Lagouira, and measures how much of the country falls in each visibility zone.

What changed recently

    Maps: moonsighting.com crescent visibility curves (OmegaHilalSighting by Fawzi Kayali, Shaukat criterion), from your folder Desktop\moon. Moroccan outcomes: Ministry of Habous and Islamic Affairs announcements (Hespress, SNRT News, Médias24, TelQuel, Le360) for 2025–26, and the Awqaf sighting reports logged by moonsighting.org.uk (ICOUK) for 2015–2024. The 1437 AH (2015–16) maps come from moonsighting.com, which still hosts them though its index starts at 1438. Model: green-zone and no-visibility nights use the observed rates (50 of 50 and 0 of 43). Borderline nights use a logistic model on cyan and grey shares, weighting recent years more (half-life 2 years). Weeks start on Monday.
